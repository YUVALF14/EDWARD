# Inhabit the Crown: historical and narrative contract

The active build retains the four acts of 1461–1471. The expansion adds 160 authored audiences to the existing 80, arranged as forty four-part household stories. There are 240 distinct base decisions, not 480 compulsory clicks or a claim of hundreds of unique endings. New reigns select 25 decisions per act while preserving the key historical audiences, filling each route to nine with weighted selections, and adding four complete stories from the reserve. Later draws respond to the king’s previous choices and relationships. Earlier choices also alter later context and unlock concrete responses. Letters, petitions and encounters bring practical requests to Edward through the audience flow.

## Evidence and reconstruction

Scene dates place reconstructed audiences within the historical campaign. They are not a documented day-by-day itinerary. Dialogue, domestic incidents, representative officers, every new letter, and hypothetical terms of royal decisions are fictional. The historical notes distinguish those from documented anchors. Resource values are abstract indicators, never asserted historical financial accounts.

## Narrative safeguards

- Elizabeth enters as a widow; her marriage and coronation remain distinct events.
- Gloucester’s adult portrait appears only in his later teenage service. His 1469 audience is limited household service. Correspondence during custody is identified as correspondence, rather than making him the king’s jailer or claiming he is physically in the chamber.
- George’s marriage remains an anchor. Refusing it changes the relationships and available settlements rather than silently erasing the historical event.
- The main act-three confinement concludes after the added custody stories, not before them.
- Warwick and Henry leave the living court after their deaths. Later Neville dependants are not Warwick speaking from beyond the grave.
- The final family scenes concern the infant Edward and existing daughters. No second royal son is introduced prematurely, and no 1483 chapter is added.
- Emotional scenes include amusement, pleasure, trust, embarrassment, longing, fear, bereavement and relief. They remain attached to royal work or relationships; they are not unrelated side quests.

## Systems and pacing

The council permits two initiatives per act; inspection and a progress are unavailable while in custody. A local progress is a short excursion from the current lodging and back, not an invented free-travel grand strategy map. France and Burgundian lands remain separate powers, with no English cooperation meter implying ownership.

The five new measures are available reserves, debt, credit, army readiness and information. Reserve/debt use abstract household units; other indicators and regional cooperation use 0–100. Income and upkeep occur once per represented calendar month; information ages and equipment wears. The ledger names changes and causes. Strong credit, information or readiness opens selected authored choices; readiness and information also affect the escape gamble.

Political changes and maintenance are normalised for the threefold campaign length. Logical promises, betrayal and dangerous actions still represent individual acts. Resource ceilings are gameplay boundaries, not historical measurements.

Music requests operate by act. Phrase-boundary changes do not stop scheduled notes, reset the phrase, or insert a silence when a card is rendered. Ordinary card colour, document reading, profile dialogs and local-progress views do not alter the score’s arrangement. Recorded acoustic samples and their existing licence credits are retained; this remains an original sampled arrangement, not a newly licensed continuous ensemble performance.

The expanded campaign uses a separate version-4 save and autosaves after decisions and completed initiatives. Earlier version-3 saves remain under their existing key and are not silently converted to different scene indices.
